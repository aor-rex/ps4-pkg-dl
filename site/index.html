What it does for you

  • ⬇Resume dead transfers

    Queue, pause, retry — with progress and ETA. Interrupted downloads pick up where they died instead of starting over.

  • ▤See your collection

    Browse covers with search and genres instead of a folder of cryptic filenames.

  • ◉Bring your own catalog

    Add games.json URLs or local files, switch sources on and off, refresh each one separately.

  • ◎Fill in artwork automatically

    Optional RAWG backfill: descriptions, genres, screenshots and trailers, cached on your machine.

  • ◈Fix wrong matches yourself

    Unmatched games wait in a review list with candidates. Pin the right one or ignore it for good.

  • ↻Stay current hands-free

    Update check on launch with Stable or Pre-release channels, restart-to-install, and a "What's new" note each version.

FAQ

Where does the game catalog come from?

The app ships with no catalog. You add your own games.json URL (FPKGi format) or local file under Settings → Library. Maintained sources (mirror + curated extras) live in the companion ps4-pkg-catalog repo.

Do I need a RAWG key?

Only for artwork and metadata enrichment, and it’s optional. Paste a free key from rawg.io/apidocs and press Start backfill; browsing and downloading work without it.

Why does archive.org ask for a cookie?

Some archive.org items are login-gated. The app can’t log in for you, so you supply your own login cookie under Settings → Library → archive.org login and the app reuses it for those downloads.

Where does the app store its data?

Everything lives under ~/.config/ps4-pkg-dl/ (XDG-aware): settings, the metadata cache database, your pinned/ignored matches, uploaded catalogs, and logs. Note the RAWG key and archive.org cookie are stored in plaintext — don’t share the folder.
